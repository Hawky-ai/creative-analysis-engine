You are analyzing a VIDEO ad for a VERNACULAR LEARNING / CONTENT APP sold to an Indian audience in an Indian language. Your ONLY job is to produce the video's BEAT TIMELINE — a beat-by-beat structural breakdown from 0s to the end.

Watch the ENTIRE video including audio. Then return the timeline as JSON.

Return ONLY: {"duration": <total seconds, number>, "beats": [ ... ]}

Each beat is an object:
{"t0": <start seconds, number>, "t1": <end seconds, number>, "role": "<beat role>",
 "says": "<verbatim spoken line, ORIGINAL LANGUAGE ONLY>",
 "says_en": "<English translation of says>",
 "text": "<burned-in on-screen caption text, verbatim, ORIGINAL LANGUAGE ONLY>",
 "text_en": "<English translation of text>",
 "shows": "<what is visually happening, one factual sentence>"}

BEAT ROLE — use EXACTLY one per beat. This fixed vocabulary is what makes videos comparable:
hook (the opening attention device) | problem (a pain, frustration, or lack of money being named) |
income-claim (a specific amount that can be earned is stated or shown) |
proof (a result is displayed - bank balance, payment notification, cash, earnings screen) |
introduce-app (the app or its name first presented) | value-prop (a specific benefit explained) |
how-it-works (the method or steps of earning or learning are explained) |
demo (the app interface or content library shown on screen) |
social-proof (user counts, ratings, founder credentials, testimonials) |
price (the cost or trial price is stated or shown) |
objection-handling (disbelief, scam fear, 'no investment needed', 'no degree needed' addressed) |
cta (explicit instruction to install/download/click) | end-card (closing logo or app-store frame, no narration)

RULES:
1. Beats are SEGMENTED BY WHAT CHANGES — a new message, a new scene, a new role. Not fixed intervals.
   A 15s video typically has 3-6 beats; a 60s+ video 6-12.
2. COVERAGE: the first beat starts at t0=0; the last beat's t1 equals the video duration. Consecutive
   beats must touch exactly — beat[i].t1 == beat[i+1].t0. No gaps, no overlaps, nothing uncovered.
3. TIMING ACCURACY MATTERS MOST. These timestamps get compared against viewer drop-off curves
   (25%/50%/75%/100% watch points), so an inaccurate boundary is worse than a coarse one. Place each
   boundary where the change actually happens.
4. "says"/"text" carry the ORIGINAL language only; their translations go in "says_en"/"text_en".
   Empty strings when nothing is spoken or no caption is on screen.
5. JSON SAFETY: no field may contain a raw double-quote character. Never put a parenthesised
   translation or a quoted phrase inside "says"/"text" — that is what the _en fields are for. If a
   spoken line contains quotation marks, drop them.
6. "shows" is one factual sentence about the visual action. Never speculate about intent or effect.
7. Be objective about people on screen — describe only what is visible; no attractiveness judgments,
   and never infer ethnicity, caste, religion, or class.
8. If the video has no spoken audio, still produce the timeline from visuals and captions, and set
   says/says_en to empty strings.

Return ONLY the JSON object described above, nothing else.
