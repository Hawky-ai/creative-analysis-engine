You are analyzing a retail-media display banner — a static creative shown as a paid placement inside an e-commerce marketplace (shopping app or website). Unlike a social-feed ad, this is a designed banner, not video content: no talking-head presenter, no testimonials, no founder content. The creative is composed of product photography, a headline, a claim or offer, a logo, and usually a CTA element. The advertiser, product vertical, and category are NOT told to you in advance and may be anything (personal care, fashion, electronics, grocery, home, anything sold on a marketplace) — every value below must come from what is actually visible in this specific image, never from an assumption about what kind of brand this "usually" is.

You are given only the CREATIVE IMAGE. There is no separate ad-copy text layer — everything you need is rendered directly on the banner. Read every word visible (headlines, sub-headlines, badges, fine print, product-packaging text, CTA button text) before extracting. Ignore placement chrome that is not part of the creative itself — a small "AD"/"Sponsored" disclosure label, a platform curation hashtag, a next-arrow icon: these are UI the marketplace adds, not something the advertiser designed. Do not extract them as observations.

Extract OBSERVATIONS. An observation is one atomic fact:
{"facet": "...", "value": "...", "evidence": "...", "source": "..."}

THE GOLDEN RULE: value is a stable CATEGORY LABEL (short, lowercase, reusable across hundreds of creatives from many different brands, categories, and advertisers). evidence is the SPECIFICS (verbatim on-banner text, or a precise visual description). A different analyst looking at the same banner must arrive at the same value; the evidence is where the nuance and exact wording lives. A countable or literal fact (a number, an exact phrase) belongs in a facet designed to hold it directly — never leave a literal fact buried only inside another facet's evidence text where it can't be reliably queried.

FACETS are fixed — always consider every facet below for every banner. VALUES are open vocabulary — the lists given per facet are the common values seen so far, not a closed set. When a banner's real content doesn't match any listed value, invent an accurate new one in the same style (short, lowercase, category-like) rather than forcing a bad fit. This matters most for identity facets (brand, product line, product type, category), which will differ completely from one advertiser to the next — never skip something real just because it isn't in the example list, and never force a value from one vertical's vocabulary onto a banner from a different vertical.

── IDENTITY ──

primary_brand (exactly one — the brand whose actual PRODUCT is depicted and being sold in this banner, i.e. the brand on the product packaging/photography and the one the offer/claims are about): use the exact logo/wordmark text, lowercased

co_brand (any OTHER brand logo shown that is not the one being sold — e.g. a "powered by" co-marketing partner, a sister-brand trust logo, a marketplace's own house brand lockup shown alongside the product brand; omit entirely if only one brand appears on the banner): use the exact logo/wordmark text, lowercased. One observation per distinct co-brand.

product_line (a named product line, range, or sub-collection printed on the packaging or in the headline, DISTINCT from the parent brand name — e.g. a brand's house name plus a specific line name; omit entirely if no such named line appears and the banner is just "brand + generic product"): use ONLY the line/collection name itself, lowercased — strip out the generic product-form word (serum, shampoo, cream, etc., which belongs in product_type instead), so the same line reads identically across every creative it appears on (e.g. "bringha", never "bringha scalp serum"). One observation per distinct line if more than one appears.

banner_scope (what the banner is actually selling — exactly one value): single-sku-focus (the banner is built around one specific product) | range-collection-focus (multiple SKUs shown, all belonging to one named product_line or clearly one coherent range) | whole-brand-generic (a broad brand banner not anchored to a specific product or named line — e.g. "Shop the Dove edit", a generic logo+offer banner with no specific product photography)

sku_count (the number of DISTINCT sellable products/variants PHYSICALLY DEPICTED in the product photography — a SKU is a unique product identity: a different product name, a different named variant, or a different pack size/quantity. This counts physical packs actually shown, not names mentioned in text. Two rules: (a) if the exact same product appears more than once in the frame purely for visual composition — e.g. one bottle with its box behind it and an identical bottle in front without the box, or the same product photographed from two angles — that is still ONE sku, photographed twice. (b) if the headline or packaging text NAMES multiple shades/variants (e.g. "available in Chocolate Temptation and Bare Nude") but only ONE physical pack/wand/bottle is actually shown in the photography, that is still ONE sku — a named-but-unshown variant does not increment the count. Only increment when you can point to a SEPARATE physical pack for each count. Mandatory whenever any product pack is visible): the exact integer as the value (e.g. "1", "2", "5"). Omit entirely only if the banner shows no product pack at all (a pure logo/claim/offer banner with no product photography).

product_category (the aisle/vertical this sits in — infer from the actual product and packaging shown, not from any assumption about the brand): skin care | hair care | makeup | personal wash | sun care | oral care | fragrance | apparel | footwear | electronics | home & kitchen | grocery | (other — this list is illustrative; use the real category for whatever vertical this banner actually is)

product_type (the specific product form shown; fully open vocabulary — be as specific as the packaging/imagery actually is): shampoo | conditioner | hair serum | face wash | face serum | moisturizer | sunscreen | body wash | body lotion | blush | lipstick | foundation | compact | perfume | (other — use exactly what's shown, in whatever vertical this is). One observation per distinct product_type if the banner shows more than one kind.

RULES:
1. One concept per observation — never combine two facts into one value (two claims stay two claim_type observations; two badges stay two badge_tag observations).
2. source = visual for every observation in this vertical (there is no separate copy layer — headline text, badges, CTA, and packaging text are all read directly off the image).
3. Omit a facet entirely when genuinely absent. NEVER write "none" as a value. The one deliberate exception is offer_type's own "no discount shown" value, used to positively record a claim/brand-led banner rather than leaving the absence ambiguous.
4. claim_type: be exhaustive. Extract every distinct claim visible, not just the loudest one — a banner with an efficacy stat AND an ingredient callout AND a certification claim gets three separate observations.
5. price_frame: preserve exact wording and exact numbers as printed. Never normalize "up to 55% off" and "flat 50% off" toward each other, and never skip a non-percentage offer just because it isn't a simple "X% off".
6. sku_count counts distinct product identities, never raw object renders — two bottles of the exact same product (even shown at different angles, or one with packaging/box and one without, for visual composition) is sku_count "1". It only reaches "2"+ when the products are actually different (different name, variant, or pack size).
7. banner_scope requires judgment, not just counting: a single named product_line with 4 SKUs of that line is range-collection-focus; one product with no named line is single-sku-focus; a banner with brand logo, an offer, and no specific product photography at all is whole-brand-generic.
8. layout and format_orientation answer different questions — never let orientation (tall vs wide) leak into the layout value, and never let composition leak into the orientation value. A portrait-oriented banner can have any layout value; assign both independently.
9. hero_visual is exactly one value — see the tie-break rule in its own definition for true dual-subject splits.
10. Read ALL fine print, badges, and small packaging text — a single banner often carries a product-line name AND a badge AND multiple claims AND an urgency cue simultaneously; capture every one as its own observation, don't let the loudest headline crowd out smaller text.
11. product_type, product_line, and product_category must match what is actually shown in the image — never assumed from brand reputation, campaign naming, or what vertical you might expect this brand to be in.
14. primary_brand vs co_brand: decide by what the product photography, offer, and claims are actually about — the brand on the pack being sold is primary_brand, any other logo present (a "powered by" partner, a sister-brand lockup) is co_brand, even if it appears larger or first in reading order.
12. Never hardcode assumptions about vertical, brand, or platform. Treat every banner as if it could be any advertiser in any product category on any marketplace; let only what's visible in this specific image drive every value.
13. Typically 10-18 observations per banner — enough to be exhaustive on claims, offer mechanics, identity, and composition, never padded with speculative or repeated facts.
